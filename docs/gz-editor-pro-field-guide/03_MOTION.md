# Motion editing on a phone

A person imports a portrait video because they want to keep a moment. They trim the lead-in, press Reverse, drag Exposure and scrub to a face. Their thumb stops. The face at that new time should appear. When they tap Play, the next pictures should move in the chosen direction, at the chosen speed, through the entire edit. They can make a second edit, save their work, come back tomorrow and export the same animation. This is the unit of product acceptance. A moving counter can coexist with a frozen picture. A correct exported file can coexist with a broken editing experience. Measure both.

A useful contract is a single edit timeline with an explicit mapping from output presentation time to source time or frame identity. Source presentation timestamps, the playback clock and GIF delay ticks are distinct quantities. Keep the project in precise units through the edit and convert to the selected output format at the boundary. For example, nominal 30 fps cannot be encoded as identical whole centisecond delays without drift. A cumulative rounding scheme can distribute 3- and 4-tick delays and keep total duration near the intended value. Preserve deliberate holds and imported variable delays. At faster than 100 distinct pictures per second, GIF has fewer positive timing ticks than requested pictures. Cumulative rounding cannot overcome that limit. For new exports, surface a representable schedule and resample at declared boundaries when the requested cadence exceeds it. Repeating an image to implement a specified hold remains intentional. For existing GIFs with zero or very short delays, distinguish original delay fields, preview pacing and exported timing; preserve bytes in the eligible unchanged-file path. Different external players may clamp those delays. Quantization can change RGB values, so verify colors with a declared tolerance and geometry with an independent motion marker. The GIF89a specification defines the delay, palette, transparency and disposal fields. [GIF89a, Graphics Control Extension and image blocks](https://www.w3.org/Graphics/GIF/spec-gif89a.txt)

### A reproducible experiment for the freeze

1. Pick the exact last-known affected build and candidate, record their source/build identities, device model, API, codec, and a content hash for a test-owned media fixture. Keep owner media private if supplied. Record actual dimensions, rotation, stream duration, frame timestamps and keyframe spacing. Use an off-center moving object with a marker that remains identifiable after monochrome and exposure changes. Include a real camera clip with different characteristics. Keep the historical portrait sequence with its original parameters when available.
2. Run the same sequence through the ordinary UI, first on the baseline and then on the candidate. Include neutral playback, trim, Reverse, B&W, Exposure, two opposite scrubs, Play through the edited end, another edit, a second play, save, project reopen, export, file reopen and play. Log the actual resulting settings from each step. Add the original user's clip if available. Record the comparable fixture result immediately if access to that clip is the only missing input.
3. For preview, sample the **visible fitted image** using the known content transform, accounting for letterbox gutters, rotation and crop. For a moving fixture, correlate visible markers against an independently generated reference sequence. Record input-to-first-correct-picture, longest unintended freeze, intervals between presented pictures, duration from first to last, and expected/actual identity at several interior positions and at the end. Check the entire playback interval using presentation trace plus visible samples. Give a uniform region, intentional hold or dropped preview frame its appropriate interpretation.
4. The historical v0.12.4 report describes an 88-frame export, two endpoint checks, and five distinct sampled preview pictures. Keep those observations as a regression baseline. A middle section could repeat indefinitely and still produce the expected two endpoints. For export, record dimensions, full ordered delays, total duration and loop metadata. Independently decode and composite **all frames** of the short fixture, compare marker positions and representative color patches, and play the reopened result. Keep selected output resolution and frame schedule fixed in the before/after run. For a long fixture, inspect full timing plus frequent samples, several interior motion transitions and both endpoints. Test a second export after toggling the look back to neutral.
5. Test cancellation while decoding, editing and writing. Rotate, background and return while paused and while playing. Supersede a slow scrub with another, then exit. Observe memory recovery across repeat cycles and verify the next project starts with its own pixels. Track a provider's irreversible copy stage distinctly so a reported cancellation corresponds to the actual output. Retain permissions and project recipes through recoverable storage errors.
6. Write down the first failed assertion in terms of what a person saw. For example, “After the final scrub the counter advanced 0.9 s while the marker stayed at its old position for 12 observations.” This narrows the next engineering choice. A codec state transition or green test count supplies context. Closure requires replaying the failed customer journey on the repaired build.

### Choosing a repair with evidence

Capture a timeline from input through scheduler, decode request, actual decoded timestamp, render, display and export. A process-local trace can use synthetic media IDs and bounded events. Record presentation gaps, request generations, decoder opens/seeks, frames decoded or discarded, queue depths, and in-flight memory. Track user-visible timestamps with a monotonic clock. If a stale generation presents, fix ownership where competing work converges. If the intended frame is decoded and never displayed, inspect the surface, bitmap ownership and state publication. If no decoded output arrives, inspect codec input/drain, flushes, end-of-stream and frame budgeting. A fallback that progresses on one emulator needs evaluation on the physical decoder that originally failed. [Android MediaCodec](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/MediaCodec) · [Android system tracing](https://developer.android.com/topic/performance/tracing)

Sequential forward requests often benefit from maintaining decoder position. For reverse or random access, one candidate is seeking to the preceding sync sample, decoding forward to the requested region, and serving a bounded window in output order. It competes with other strategies on measured latency, memory and fidelity. On reordered or variable-rate input, make selections from presentation timestamps. Avoid assuming that decoding order, frame index and presentation order coincide. Bounded scratch intervals can help reverse export on a long clip. Check cancellation and cleanup on both sides of blocking work. Decoder configuration, codec flush and buffer ownership can differ by route. Codex can replace the route if that produces a simpler verified implementation. [Android MediaExtractor](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/MediaExtractor) · [Android MediaCodec](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/MediaCodec)

Budget and validate an edit before committing it. A user who widens a trim after setting a fixed frame rate should see the supported choice or an explanation that preserves the current valid edit. Try just below, at, and just above each supported duration/frame-count limit, using the actual frame schedule and safe arithmetic. Repeat the action from a reopened legacy project. Verify that cancel, retry and a later valid trim still work.

### Export speed without a hidden quality cut

The supplied transcript gives an earlier 2.95-second portrait export at 77.1 s on API 29 and 39.1 s on API 35. These are historical emulator observations. PR #20 changes the forward/export path and enforces a 15-second emulator ceiling on this fixture. It stops the Save timer when the saved-file action appears, whereas the prior log also included reopening the bytes and parsing metadata. Keep the older figures as context. For an exact speedup, collect the same time boundaries on both builds. The checkpoint also records a longer five-picture preview sample on Android 15. Investigate that playback result separately with repeated, paired runs; export improvement alone does not explain it. The reported results add emulator evidence. Handset experience on the original clip still needs its own observation. [PR #20, production and test changes](https://github.com/MrSkyrimFan/GZ-Mobile-001/pull/20)

Time from the user's confirmation to readable output, and time the encoder separately. Name the fixtures and build references. Keep fixture hash, source and output dimensions, frame count, palette settings, decoder route, trim, speed, selected effects and storage destination in each measurement. Different devices, or even different provider types, warrant separate rows.

Measure decode/seek, conversion and scaling, edit rendering, palette estimation/mapping, compression, and destination copy. With overlapping stages include wall time as well as stage occupancy so totals are interpreted correctly. Record seek and decoded-frame counts, peak process memory, scratch disk use and thermal state. Try several paired baseline/candidate runs on the same device, with cold and warm results separate. Show individual timings and the median; reserve percentile claims for a sufficiently large sample. If a speedup comes from reducing needless seeks, the count and decode duration should fall while dimensions, timing, output markers and color quality stay within their original tolerances. A faster run with skipped interior movement is a failed trade.

Use the previously proposed mainstream-phone response budgets where comparable, for example first moving picture at p95 within 750 ms and a settled warm 720p scrub at p95 within 200 ms. These are **proposed product targets**, sensitive to device and fixture. Report longest stalls as well as percentiles. For the short export, record measured seconds and a speed factor equal to export wall time divided by edited duration. The earlier five-second target for a *different* 720p60-to-180×320 fixture must not be borrowed as a measurement for the 1080×1920 portrait source. Calibrate the portrait target on named supported phones and real media, with the owner’s intended quality settings. Keep improving a slow path while running independent acceptance work.

## Video after the four editing workspaces have been accepted

Video's organizing principle is **what changes, on which track, during which time interval**. A user can make a cut on day one. An experienced editor can place music, mute dialogue for part of a clip, keyframe a moving privacy region, grade clips and export a clean mix. Keep the app's visual language and touch conventions. Show detailed controls at the selected clip, region or audio track. A shared B&W or Punch transform can operate on timed frames while photographic catalog controls remain in Regular. GIF tools operate on silent animation.

| Capability and scope | Path from first useful result to professional depth | Concrete acceptance case |
|---|---|---|
| Timeline and splicing | Import multiple clips and local audio; scrub, zoom, split, trim, reorder and join; add ripple operations, track locking, snapping, markers and frame stepping. Preserve stable clip identities and the distinction between source and project time. | Assemble A and B with visible cut markers. Split B, shorten A, undo and redo. Check export frame immediately before and after each cut and the new position of every downstream edit. No unexplained black boundary frames. |
| Layers and transitions | Add overlays, timed titles, opacity and transform keyframes. Add transitions with defined duration, overlap, interpolation and audio behavior. Support precise placement while previews may use proxies. | Overlay a locator for exactly two seconds, animate one corner during a dissolve, then export. Sample inside and outside its time interval. Check midpoint opacity and endpoint positions against reference frames. |
| Region edits | Blur or pixelate a selected shape on a specified interval; position it by keyframe, feather where appropriate and allow correction between keyframes. More advanced tracking can be qualified against the same contract. | Follow a moving plate over a cut; inspect all targeted frames at final output resolution. Verify redaction covers the target at both ends, stays off adjacent content, respects rotation and ends exactly where requested. If the intent is concealment, the chosen effect strength must achieve it. |
| Color and finishing | Offer timing-scoped original/B&W/Punch and compatible looks, strength and undo. Qualify temporal grading, matching, curves, scopes and HDR handling as supported. | Apply B&W only to B. Compare a chart and skin tones in A, B and the transition. Reopen and export; inspect decoded colors and actual primaries, transfer, range and rotation metadata. |
| Audio/music | Import a local file, position and trim it, mute a selected range of original sound, mix levels and fade. Expand to multitrack, keyframed gain, ducking, waveform editing and voiceover as qualified. | Source contains a 440-Hz test tone, music an 880-Hz tone. Mute the source only on [2 s, 4 s). Inspect independently decoded audio away from boundaries for source suppression and continued music. Check gain ramps and adjacent unmuted regions; align a reference flash/click at beginning and end within one output frame. |
| Project and delivery | Save the editable timeline; export a rendered file with declared frame rate, dimensions, audio, orientation and color policy. Probe the device's supported encoders; expose options as their complete paths qualify. | Relink a missing song, recover a project after process death, export with local music, inspect the finished container and play it in another player. Repeat low-storage and cancelled-export cases without damaging the last save. |

**Worked studio session.** A is four seconds, B is six. Join them with a one-second visual dissolve, producing a nine-second timeline. Place an eight-second song at project time one second. Mute only B's source audio at project times 4–6 seconds. Apply Punch to A and B&W to B, track a mosaic on B between project times 5–8 seconds, and export in the chosen size. The song should run all the way to the end and remain audible through the source mute. Reopen the project, move B’s in-point, and review the displayed policy for ripple and anchoring before accepting. Export again, checking overlap, silence only on the requested source, synchronization, effect bounds, orientation and color in an independent player. This single exercise catches contradictions between several individually green tools.

A composition with explicit media references, per-track edit intervals and one source-to-project time mapping can feed both preview and export. Android Media3's composition, preview and transformation capabilities are candidate building blocks. Verify support in the version installed, against the required transitions, mixed rates, simultaneous video tracks and audio semantics. Codex may extend or choose another qualified local pipeline. Distinguish preview proxy fidelity from final rendering. Share transformations and immutable media identity across modes where useful while each mode retains its own document semantics. [Android Media3 multi-asset editing](https://developer.android.com/media/media3/transformer/multi-asset)

For long programs, include fractional or variable-rate sources and mixed audio sample rates. Place a flash/click near the beginning, after a speed or splice edit and near the end of a ten-minute timeline. Independently demux, decode and measure drift. Test silent, mono and stereo inputs and missing audio tracks. Handle unsupported encodes before committing to a long job, keep the timeline recoverable across interruption, and show meaningful progress based on measured frame or stage completion. Physical performance qualification should include thermal slowdown, UI responsiveness during export and a second job after cancelling the first.
